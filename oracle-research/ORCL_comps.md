## Provenance — read this before using any cell

**`[C]` = consensus or reported, taken directly from the source.**
Prices, enterprise value, market cap, book value, trailing metrics, and the **next fiscal year (FY+1)** consensus for revenue, gross margin, operating income, net income, EPS and free cash flow.

**`[E]` = my own estimate, not consensus.**
**FY2028 (FY+2) consensus is paywalled at this source for every name in both sets.** Calendarising to a common NTM window requires blending FY+1 and FY+2, so **the FY+2 leg of every calendarised multiple is mine.** The rule is mechanical and disclosed rather than judgemental:

> `g(FY+2) = 0.60 × g(FY+1) + 0.40 × 6.0%` — FY+1 consensus growth decayed 40% of the way toward a 6% long-run rate.
> EPS, operating income and FCF grow at that rate plus 300bp of operating leverage (150bp for CRWV and NBIS, which have no operating leverage to speak of yet).
> EBITDA is FY+1 operating income plus FY0 D&A scaled by revenue growth.

I have no way to know how far FY+2 consensus sits from this rule. **Treat every `[E]` column as directionally useful and precisely wrong.** If you get terminal access, replacing the FY+2 leg with real consensus is the single highest-value upgrade to this table.

**Negative and near-zero growth is reported `n/m`, never as a printed number.** CRWV and NBIS carry negative NTM EPS, so their P/E and PEG are `n/m` by construction, not by omission.

### Metrics the capital structure invalidates for ORCL

**EV/FCF and P/FCF are `n/m`** — free cash flow is deeply negative, so any FCF-based screen simply drops Oracle. That is the real distortion.

**ROE and P/B are NOT broken, contrary to the working brief.** At 31 May 2026 total Oracle stockholders' equity was **$42,508m** (10-K R2), up from $20,451m a year earlier: paid-in capital of $43,243m plus $4,954m of preferred against an accumulated deficit of only $(4,309)m. ROE is **53.4%** and P/B **11.7x**, and both are meaningful. Book equity was negative around FY2022; two years of retained earnings and the near-total halt in buybacks ($95m in FY2026) have restored it.

**What the table says.** On EV/EBITDA — the metric the brief asks me to lead with — Oracle at **11.6x is the second-cheapest name in this set**, behind only CRM at 11.0x and against a peer median of **15.3x**. It simultaneously carries **net debt/EBITDA of 5.0x against a peer median of 2.4x**, and **capex at 83% of revenue against a peer median of 2%**. Those three facts sit together and they are the entire debate: you are being offered a discounted multiple on a business that has temporarily stopped resembling its comp set.

### Why CoreWeave and Nebius are shown but are not comparables

Both screen at roughly **5x EV/EBITDA**, which looks cheap and is not. EV/EBIT is **54.6x** for CRWV and `n/m` for NBIS; NTM FCF margins are **−217%** and **−542%**; capex is **401%** and **2,103%** of revenue. EBITDA taken before a capex line of that size is not a proxy for cash, earnings, or anything else. They are included because the brief asked for them and because their existence is itself the finding — the "tier two" cohort Oracle now gets grouped with is not valued on multiples by anyone.

### The comparison that does matter

**Capex as a percentage of revenue: ORCL 83%, MSFT 35%, GOOGL 33%, AMZN 24%.** Oracle is spending more relative to its own revenue base than any established hyperscaler, while carrying materially more leverage and generating a fraction of the cash flow. Oracle's balance sheet is being asked to do something Microsoft's and Alphabet's cash flows do without noticing.

## PEG — three bases, never one number

At $151.94 against FY2027E non-GAAP EPS of $8.05 (**guidance**, and also the consensus average of 42 analysts — low $7.72 / high $8.57), the FY2027E P/E is **18.9x**.

| Growth basis | Growth | PEG on FY27E P/E (18.9x) | PEG on NTM P/E (17.8x) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FY26→FY27 off the **$7.63 headline** — contaminated by the Ampere and Bloom Energy gains | 5.50% | **3.43** | 3.23 |
| FY26→FY27 off the **$6.83 clean base** — management's own like-for-like | 17.86% | **1.06** | 1.00 |
| FY27E→FY28E `[E]` — FY28 consensus is paywalled at source | 24.98% | **0.76** | 0.71 |

**The one I would defend is 1.06.** The $6.83 base is the one Oracle itself uses for its "+18%" FY2027 growth claim (press release footnote 1, 10 June 2026), so it is the only like-for-like comparison management has actually sanctioned. The 3.43 is arithmetically correct and economically misleading. The 0.76 rests on my FY2028 estimate, not consensus.

**A methodological note.** Mixing an NTM P/E numerator with a FY26→FY27 growth denominator double-counts: NTM EPS already blends FY27 and FY28, so it embeds part of the acceleration you are then dividing by. The FY27E P/E column is the internally consistent one, and it is the one to quote.

**Salesforce:** CRM's FY+1 EPS growth is positive on this vintage of data, so its PEG is computable — but note that CRM moved +22.6% on 27 August on its Q2 print and its estimates had not been revised at the time of this pull. **The CRM row is the least stable in the table.** Where forward growth is negative or near zero the entry is `n/m`, by design.

## Source-date hygiene

Aggregator update dates differ by name even when prices are same-day. Everything here was pulled in a single session on 27–28 August 2026; no figure was carried over from an earlier vintage. Every price was cross-checked against a live quote before use — ORCL's $151.94 was confirmed against the 27 August close and its $345.72 52-week high, and the 52-week **low is $114.50 intraday** (a working brief figure of $118.86 is the lowest *close*, a different basis).
